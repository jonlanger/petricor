# Ergonomics, grips & touch points

Source tags: **[CCOHS]** Canadian Centre for Occupational Health & Safety, hand-tool design · **[EW]** Ergoweb controls guide · **[RoT]** rule of thumb — validate with prototypes and, for anything critical, anthropometric data (e.g. percentile hand data for your market).

## Grips
- Power grip diameter 30–50 mm, ~40 mm optimal [CCOHS]. Precision grip 8–16 mm, ~12 mm typical [CCOHS].
- Handle length ≥100 mm, ~120 mm recommended; longer for gloves [CCOHS].
- Two-handle span 65–90 mm; avoid >100 mm [CCOHS].
- Weight: one-hand general ≤1.4 kg; precision ≤0.4 kg [CCOHS]. For handheld CE aim far lower (remotes ~60–120 g, controllers ~200–300 g [RoT]).
- Surfaces: non-slip, compressible, not glossy on grip zones [CCOHS] → TPE overmold, fine-to-medium texture (Mold-Tech C-series class), or soft-touch paint.
- Oval/elliptical sections resist rotation in hand better than round [RoT].
- Balance: center of mass inside the grip zone; heavy battery near the palm.

## Buttons & controls
- Actuation force < ~1 kg (≈10 N) [EW]; consumer tactile domes are commonly ~1.6–3.5 N with 0.2–0.5 mm travel; mechanical keys ~0.5–0.7 N with 2–4 mm [RoT — check switch datasheets].
- Minimum finger-press target ~9–10 mm, thumb ~15 mm+ [RoT]; spacing ≥ 3–5 mm edge-to-edge or a physical separator [RoT].
- Cap proud 0.3–0.8 mm (findable, not snaggy) or recessed with a clear edge for guards against accidental press [RoT].
- Cap-to-opening gap 0.10–0.20 mm per side, constant all round.
- Give primary controls a distinct shape or texture so they're found without looking (concave dish, raised dot, ridged edge).

## Affordance vocabulary (form → meaning)
| Form cue | Reads as |
|---|---|
| Concave dish | "Press here" (fingertip seat) |
| Convex, proud, domed | "Grip / hold" or a primary button |
| Knurl / ridges around a cylinder | "Rotate" |
| Linear ridges on a flat | "Slide" in the ridge-normal direction |
| Notch / thumb scoop | "Pry / open here" |
| Soft/TPE zone | "Hold here" |
| Color accent | "This is the one thing to touch" (use once) |
| Light / translucency | Status, not touch — keep away from grip zones |

## Touch-point checklist per product
1. List every contact: hold, press, rotate, plug, open, charge, clean, set down.
2. For each: which finger/palm, how often, eyes-on or eyes-free, force, feedback (click, detent, light, sound).
3. Assign form + CMF per contact from the table above.
4. Check first-touch: when someone picks it up in the dark, what do they feel first? It should be the grip zone, not a seam or a screw.
5. Check put-down: stable on a table? Feet/soft base? Does it roll? Does it scratch furniture?
