# Brand design-language notes

These are observational summaries of widely publicized products to steer form language. They are not retrieved facts about specific products — before quoting a spec, material, or date, verify it with a search and cite what you retrieved. Borrow **principles**; never reproduce a signature product, logo, or trade dress.

## Precision & honest tech
**Apple** — Unibody/CNC aluminum and glass, continuous-curvature (squircle-like) corners, flush or near-flush seams, few visible fasteners, restrained color with seasonal accents, obsessive gap consistency. Borrow: G2 corners (`plan="squircle"`), constant tiny gaps, one material per face.

**Nothing** — Transparency as brand: see-through backs revealing arranged internals, white/black/grey palette with red accent, dot-matrix type, light as an interface element. Borrow: making internal layout a designed surface (align screws, covers, flex routing), a single red accent.

**Teenage Engineering** — Playful-technical instruments: anodized aluminum or molded bodies, grid-disciplined control layouts, bold color-coded keys/knobs, exposed screws and PCBs treated as graphics, tiny precise typography. Borrow: grid discipline, color as function coding, honest fasteners.

## Calm, reductive
**Braun (Dieter Rams era)** — "Less, but better": rational grids, rounded rectangles, perforated grille fields, off-white/grey bodies with one colored control (famously green/yellow/orange accents), clear operating hierarchy. Borrow: perforation grids aligned to the layout grid, one accent.

**Muji** — Anonymous, quiet, white/grey/natural materials, no decoration, generous radii, products that disappear into the home. Borrow: removal until only function remains; natural textures.

**Bang & Olufsen** — Architectural, sculptural geometry; aluminum with fabric/wood/leather; motion as theatre; premium mechanical details (knurls, precise rotary controls). Borrow: material contrasts, crafted rotary controls, audio as furniture.

## Soft tech / home
**Sonos** — Monolithic forms, matte black/white, perforated or fabric grilles, capacitive top controls with minimal markings, focus on room-fit. Borrow: continuous grille wraps, subtle top-surface controls.

**Google (Nest)** — Fabric-covered soft forms, pebble-like shapes, pastel/soft neutrals, light-ring feedback, recycled materials marketed prominently. Borrow: fabric over frame, light as status, friendly radii.

**Logitech** — Ergonomics-led peripherals: sculpted grips, rubberized zones, thumb rests, clear touch-point hierarchy. Borrow: ergonomic sculpting driven by hand posture; soft/hard material zoning.

## Engineered / rugged
**Dyson** — Engineering made visible: exposed cyclones, colored functional parts (e.g. nickel/purple/yellow), transparent bins, geometric precision, the mechanism is the aesthetic. Borrow: color-coding the working parts, transparent windows onto function.

**DeWalt** — Yellow/black brand block, heavy TPE overmold on grips and bumpers, ribbed texture, strong protective geometry at impact points, jobsite toughness. Borrow: bumpers at corners, overmold where hands and floors hit.

**Festool** — Pro-tool system thinking: green/charcoal, modular docking (Systainer), precise interfaces, clean surfaces with purposeful ribs. Borrow: system interfaces, docking geometry, purposeful ribbing.

## Using lineages
Pick one primary and at most one secondary lineage per product, and state the blend ("Braun rationality + Nothing transparency"). Then translate into 3–5 concrete rules (radius family, accent budget, grille pattern, seam treatment, fastener visibility) and check the model against them.
